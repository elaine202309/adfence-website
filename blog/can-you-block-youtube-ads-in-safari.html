Can you block YouTube ads in Safari?

The ad blocker cleans up a news site, then you open YouTube and a 30-second pre-roll plays. That is not a broken setting — it is where the ad lives.

The short answer, without the usual hedging: not the ads inside the video. A Safari content blocker works by refusing requests and hiding page elements, and a pre-roll does not look like either. It is part of the same media stream as the video you clicked on, assembled on YouTube's servers and delivered from YouTube's own address. There is no separate request to refuse and no element to hide — it is simply not a separate thing.

So when a blocker that makes the rest of the web quiet does nothing here, nothing is actually wrong. It is doing exactly what it can reach, and this is the part it can't.

The short version: in-video ads — pre-rolls, mid-rolls, the sponsored clips between chapters — cannot be blocked by a Safari content blocker. What can be quieted down is everything around the player: banners, promoted shelves, panels, the comment clutter. Other video sites are often different, because many of them request their ads separately.

Why the ad is not a separate request

A banner ad on a news site is a request to another company's server. Refuse that request and the space stays empty. A YouTube pre-roll is not like that. YouTube stitches it into the video response on its own side, so by the time the stream reaches your iPhone, the ad and the video are one continuous thing from one address. A filter list can only say "do not load this address" or "hide this element". Neither applies.

The same is true inside the app and in the browser — the app and youtube.com receive the same stream. The 30-second wait exists in the video itself, not in a box drawn over it.

What AdFence does on a YouTube page

The page around the player is ordinary web: images, scripts, panels and recommended-video shelves, most of which come from places a filter list already knows. With the extensions enabled, that part gets noticeably quieter — fewer promoted rows, no banner across the top, less tracking around the page. Reload the page after changing any switch so the new rules apply.

It is worth being clear about what this is and is not. It is a cleaner page and less tracking around a video; it is not an ad-free player, and no setting in the app will make it one.

The YouTube app is not Safari

On iPhone and iPad, the YouTube app runs outside Safari and cannot use Safari extensions at all. Neither can the browser that opens when you tap a YouTube link inside another app. The extension only exists inside Safari, so if you are watching in the app, none of this applies — including the page cleanup. If you want the quieter page, open youtube.com in Safari.

Other video sites are often different

YouTube is not every video site. Plenty of publishers rent their player and its ads from someone else, and the ad arrives as a separate request to an ad server — exactly the kind of request a filter list can refuse. The quick way to tell them apart:

  • The ad is a separate clip with its own countdown and player UI — often blockable, because something external had to load.
  • The ad arrives as part of one continuous stream, and the player behaves as if nothing changed — stitched in, like YouTube, and not blockable from Safari.

That is why a blocker can make one site ad-free and leave another untouched, without any setting being wrong.

Tuning a single site

If a page keeps showing one element you would rather not see, AdFence has per-site tools. Open Site Settings for that address: Pause Blocking steps back entirely, and Custom Element Blocker lets you add a single element instead. The rules you create collect on the Custom tab, where you can review or remove them.

If ads you expected to disappear are still there

The in-video case is settled, but other ads should be going. Check these in order — the 30-second extension check comes first:

  1. The extension is on in Safari. Safari keeps its own switch, and updates can turn it off.
  2. The master switch is on. Blocking at the top of the General tab has to be on; while it is off, every extension loads an empty rule list.
  3. The site is paused. The Custom tab lists every paused site; anything there stays paused until removed.
  4. You are in Safari, not an in-app browser. Links from other apps open their own browser, where no extension runs.
  5. The filter lists are older than the site's latest change. Lists update with app updates, not continuously.

What actually removes the pre-rolls

On iPhone, iPad and Mac, the dependable way is YouTube's own paid tier, which removes ads in the app and on the site. That is a fact about how the video is delivered, not a shortcoming of this app: a content blocker is a rules file Safari applies to requests and page elements, and the inside of a video stream is neither. If an app in the App Store promises otherwise, look closely at what it is actually changing — it is not doing it with Safari's content-blocking APIs.

What AdFence can do is make the rest of the web quiet, including the page around a video. That is a smaller claim than some advertising makes, and it is the one that holds up.

Frequently asked questions

Does AdFence block YouTube ads?

It blocks the advertising around a video — banners, promoted shelves, panels and the clutter of the page — but not the pre-roll and mid-roll ads inside the video itself. Those are part of the same stream as the video, so there is no separate request for a content blocker to refuse.

Why do some people say a Safari blocker removes YouTube ads?

Sometimes they mean the page around the player, which does get quieter. Other times they are describing a different kind of tool — a browser or a traffic filter outside Safari — which is not what a Safari content blocker is or can be.

Does using the YouTube website instead of the app help?

Not for the in-video ads. The website and the app receive the same stream, and a content blocker cannot alter it. The website does benefit from the page cleanup, which the app cannot offer at all because it does not run Safari extensions.

Is there anything that removes the pre-rolls?

On iPhone and iPad, the reliable way is YouTube's own paid tier, which removes ads in the app and on the web. Safari extensions cannot, and an app that claims to is either doing something outside the content-blocking model or describing a feature that does not hold up.

Are ads on other video sites different?

Often, yes. Many sites request their video ads separately from an ad server, and those requests can be refused like any other. The tell is whether the ad is a separate clip with its own countdown or arrives as part of one continuous stream.
