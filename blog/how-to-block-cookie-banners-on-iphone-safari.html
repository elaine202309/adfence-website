How to block cookie banners on iPhone Safari

You press Reject all, scroll to the next page, and the same panel slides up again. Cookie notices are hidden by a different switch from ads — this is the one to flip.

A cookie banner is not an ad. That sounds obvious, and it is the reason the usual "block ads" switch does not always make it go away. An ad is a request to somebody else's server, which a filter can refuse. A consent panel is usually part of the page itself — a script from a consent vendor, or plain markup with a dark overlay behind it — so removing it means hiding elements that have already arrived.

AdFence does both, with two separate switches: the filters handle requests, and Hide Cookie Notices handles the panel. If you are only seeing banners, the second one is the one you want.

The short version: open AdFence, stay on the General tab, scroll to Other Settings, and make sure Hide Cookie Notices is on. Reload the page in Safari. If one particular site stops working afterwards, allow its notice back for that address only — the steps are below.

What a cookie banner actually is

Almost every panel you meet is one of three things, and they behave differently:

  • A consent dialog from a third-party script. Most large publishers rent one. The name on the banner — OneTrust, Cookiebot, Didomi, Quantcast and the like — builds the panel, dims the page and locks scrolling. That script and its stylesheet have recognisable addresses, so both the request and the panel can be caught.
  • First-party markup with an overlay. The same idea, written by the site itself: a fixed box at the bottom of the screen and a full-width layer behind it. Nothing external to block; the elements are simply hidden.
  • A consent wall. The article genuinely is withheld until you answer, on the server. No content blocker can bring back what the site refuses to send.

Hide Cookie Notices is for the first two. Hiding the panel also gives the page back its scrolling and its taps, which is the part you notice most on a phone.

Switching it on

  1. Open AdFence and stay on the General tab.
  2. Scroll to Other Settings and switch on Hide Cookie Notices.
  3. Return to Safari and reload the page.

It is deliberately its own switch. Plenty of people want the ads gone and are happy to answer a consent question once; others would rather never see the panel at all. Keeping the two apart means neither choice costs the other.

Why the same banner keeps coming back

Three reasons, and only one of them is a fault:

  • You never answered it. A hidden dialog is still unanswered. Many sites store "no answer yet" and show the panel again on the next page or the next visit — so it is hidden again each time rather than being remembered as dismissed.
  • Consent is stored per site, not per person. A newspaper's article pages, its video section and its shop can be three different sites as far as the browser is concerned, each with its own stored answer. Clearing website data, or opening a private window, resets all of them.
  • The site changed the panel. Consent vendors ship new versions with new class names on a rolling basis. The shipped rules match the older shape until the next AdFence release carries the newer one — that lag follows the app's update schedule, and is not a setting you got wrong.

There is also a half-second version of this: the panel can flash before it disappears. The hiding rules are applied as the page renders, so on a slow connection the dialog may be visible for a moment first. It is the same rule doing its job, just later than you would like.

When a site needs its banner

Some sites treat the panel as a gate: answer it and the article appears; hide it and you get greyed-out text you cannot scroll. Others keep the Manage preferences link in the banner, and sometimes the sign-in link lives there too. For these, do not switch the feature off — allow the notice for one address:

  1. Open Site Settings and enter the address of that site.
  2. Under Site Overrides, switch on Allow Cookie Notices.
  3. Tap Save, then reload the page in Safari.

The override applies to that address alone, and every other site keeps the notice hidden. It is the same mechanism as Pause Blocking, narrowed to one filter.

What it can't do

  • Answer for you. Hiding the dialog clicks nothing. If a site only works after you accept or reject, you still have to do that; the app removes the panel, not the decision.
  • Reach a consent wall. When the article genuinely isn't sent until you answer, no blocker can conjure it up.
  • Work outside Safari. Links opened inside Instagram, Facebook, Gmail, Reddit and most other apps use those apps' own browsers, and no Safari extension runs there. Open the page in Safari instead.

Nor is it a tracker blocker by itself: hiding the panel does not stop a script that has already loaded. Advertising and tracking requests are their own switches, and the same screen has those too.

If the banner is still there

Check these in order — the first two solve most cases:

  1. The extension is on in Safari. It is a switch inside Safari, not inside the app, and Safari can turn it off after an update. The 30-second check is in this post.
  2. The master switch is on. At the top of the General tab, Blocking has to be on as well; while it is off, every extension loads an empty rule list and nothing is hidden anywhere.
  3. The site is on the paused list. Look in the Custom tab. Anything there stays paused until you remove it, however long ago it was added.
  4. Safari's own site permission. If AdFence is set to Ask rather than Allow for that website, Safari waits for you before any extension can act on it.
  5. You are not in Safari. An in-app browser cannot run the extension at all, however the settings look.

If the panel survives all of that, it is probably from a vendor whose markup changed recently — the next AdFence update closes the gap. Until then, you can hide it yourself: Site Settings, then Custom Element Blocker, and add the element. Frequently asked questions

Does hiding the banner accept or reject cookies for me?

Neither. Hiding the panel is a display change: it clicks nothing, accepts nothing and deletes nothing already stored. If you want a site to record your choice, answer or dismiss the panel before it is hidden, or allow notices for that site under Site Overrides.

Will hiding cookie banners break a website?

Rarely, but some sites gate their content on an answer and leave the page greyed out behind the panel. For those, open Site Settings, then Site Overrides, and switch on Allow Cookie Notices for that address alone — every other site keeps the notice hidden.

Does it work on iPad and Mac as well?

Yes. Hide Cookie Notices is the same switch on iPad and in the Mac app, and Site Overrides work the same way on every platform.

Why does the banner flash for a second before it goes away?

The hiding rules are applied as the page renders, so on a slow connection the panel can be drawn for a moment before it is hidden. It is the same rule working, just later than you would like — no setting can make it earlier than the page itself.

Is hiding cookie notices the same as blocking ads?

No, and they are separate switches on purpose. Ads are requests to other servers, which filters can refuse; a cookie banner is usually part of the page, so hiding it means hiding page elements. Keeping them apart lets you choose either one.
