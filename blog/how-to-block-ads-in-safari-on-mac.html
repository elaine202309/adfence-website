How to block ads in Safari on Mac

On a Mac the blocker lives in two places: Safari decides whether to run it, and the app decides what it filters. Both have to be on, and they are easy to miss.

If you set this up on an iPhone, the path is well known: Settings → Apps → Safari → Extensions. The Mac has no equivalent step in the Settings app. Safari keeps its own preferences, and everything about which extensions run lives inside Safari itself. The AdFence app is the other place, and it holds the switches that decide what gets filtered.

That split is the most common reason a Mac can have the app installed, look correctly configured, and still show every ad. Safari runs nothing until the extension is ticked, and ticking it changes nothing about what is filtered until the app’s own switches say so.

The short version: in Safari, open Safari → Settings (⌘,) and tick AdFence in the Extensions tab. Then open the AdFence app, stay on General, and make sure Blocking is on along with the rules you want. Reload the page.

1. Turn the extension on in Safari

  1. In Safari, choose Safari → Settings from the menu bar (⌘,).
  2. Open the Extensions tab.
  3. Tick AdFence in the list on the left. The eight extensions appear as separate entries, each with its own tick.
  4. If you browse in private windows, tick Allow in Private Browsing for the extensions you want active there.

The note in the right-hand pane is worth reading once: a content blocker “does not have permission to read, alter, or transmit content from any webpages”. That is not an error. The extension supplies rule lists; Safari applies them. That is how this kind of blocker works, and it is the reason it does not need your browsing history.

2. Choose what gets filtered in the app

Open AdFence and stay on the General tab. Blocking at the top is the master switch; below it, the Blocking List holds the rules — Block Ads, Bypass Anti-Adblock, Safe Browsing, Block Trackers — and Other Settings below that holds the page-level ones, from cookie notices to pop-ups and comments. Only what is on gets filtered.

The order matters. The app writes the rules, Safari applies them, so after changing a switch, return to Safari and reload the page — a page already loaded keeps the content it fetched before the change.

Per-site controls on the Mac

Two mechanisms sit on top of the global switches, and they are easy to confuse.

  • In the app: Site Settings for an address, with Pause Blocking to stand down for that site and Site Overrides to allow a single filter. The entries collect on the Custom tab.
  • In Safari: a page can be reloaded without content blockers from the reload button’s menu. That is temporary and only for that tab — the next reload brings the blocker back.

The first is persistent; the second is an escape hatch. If you find yourself using the second one on the same site often, the first is the better tool.

What is different from the iPhone

  • The path: Safari → Settings → Extensions on the Mac, not Settings → Apps → Safari. There is no system Settings step.
  • The device: extensions are enabled per device. A Mac and an iPhone each need the tick once; after that they keep their own switches.
  • The browser: Safari extensions only run in Safari. On the Mac it is easy to check a site in Chrome or Firefox and conclude the blocker is broken — it never applied there.

If ads still appear on the Mac

Check these in order — the first two solve most cases:

  1. The extension is ticked. Open Safari’s Extensions tab again; updates and Mac migrations can leave it off.
  2. The master switch is on. Blocking in the app has to be on; while it is off, every extension loads an empty rule list.
  3. The site is not paused. Look in the app’s Custom tab.
  4. You are looking at the right browser. Ads in Chrome or Firefox are a different program, not a setting.
  5. The ad is one a content blocker cannot reach. In-video YouTube ads and links opened in other apps’ built-in browsers are the two usual cases.

If a page behaves strangely instead — a player that will not start, a form that will not submit — the per-site tools are the right answer before anything global. And if the extension itself looks off, the 30-second check is in this post.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need the app on the Mac as well as on my iPhone?

Extensions are enabled per device, so the Mac needs the app installed and its extension ticked in Safari once. The same is true for an iPad. After that, each device keeps its own switches.

Does it block ads in Chrome or Firefox on my Mac?

No. A Safari extension only runs inside Safari, so AdFence does nothing in Chrome, Firefox or any other browser on the Mac. Those browsers need their own blocker.

Does it work in private browsing windows on the Mac?

Only if the extension is allowed there. In Safari’s Extensions settings, with AdFence selected, turn on Allow in Private Browsing for each extension you want active in private windows.

Safari says the extension has no permission to read webpages. Is that normal?

Yes. A content blocker supplies rule lists that Safari applies itself; it does not read, alter or transmit page content. That sentence in the Extensions pane is describing how the technology works, not an error.

Why does one site still show ads on my Mac?

Check the site in Site Settings: if it was paused for any reason it stays paused until removed. Ads inside a video stream and ads in other apps’ browsers are separate cases that no Safari content blocker can reach.
