Safari treats ad blockers differently from almost every other kind of app. Installing one does not switch it on. The extension ships turned off, and Safari is the only place that can turn it on — not the App Store, not the app itself, and not a restart. That single detail accounts for most of the "this blocker does nothing" complaints, including the ones where it worked fine last month.

So the first thing to check is not a setting inside the app. It is a switch inside Safari.

The short version: open Settings → Apps → Safari → Extensions on iPhone or iPad, or Safari → Settings → Extensions on the Mac, and make sure the AdFence switch is on. If it was off, that was the problem — reload the page.

The 30-second check

On iPhone and iPad

  1. Open Settings, scroll down to Apps, then tap Safari.
  2. Tap Extensions, then AdFence.
  3. Turn the switch on, and allow access to the websites you want it to work on.

On the Mac

  1. In Safari, open Settings (⌘,) and go to the Extensions tab.
  2. Tick AdFence in the list on the left.
  3. Choose which sites it can access in the pane on the right.

AdFence ships eight extensions rather than one, so they each appear in that list. If at least one of them is off, the ads that extension handles will come back while everything else keeps working — which is why a blocker can look "half broken" rather than off.

Once the switch is on, go back to Safari and reload the page properly. A page that was already loaded keeps the content it fetched before the extension was running.

Already switched on? Check these four, in order

1. The site is paused in the app

AdFence has a per-site Pause Blocking switch, and once a site is on that list it stays paused until you take it off. Open the Custom tab, find the site, and check. If you added a site to get a video player working months ago, it is still paused now.

2. The extension can't see that site

Safari has its own per-site permission, separate from anything the app does. If AdFence is set to Ask rather than Allow for a website, Safari waits for you to grant access, and until you do, the blocker has nothing to work with. The same screen that shows the on/off switch, a level down, lists every site you have given or refused access to; delete the entry to be asked again.

3. You aren't actually in Safari

Taps on links inside Instagram, Facebook, Gmail, Reddit and most other apps open a browser built into that app, not Safari. Those browsers do not support Safari extensions at all, so no ad blocker of this kind can reach them. This is a limit of how the app is built, not a setting you have missed. The fix is to open the page in Safari — usually the share menu, then Open in Safari — or paste the address into the address bar yourself.

4. The ad isn't in a filter list

A content blocker does not look at a page and decide what looks like an ad. It matches the requests and page elements it knows about against filter lists that ship inside the app, and it is only as good as those lists. Plenty of advertising falls outside them:

  • Sponsored posts written to look like the site's own content.
  • Ads served from the site's own domain, with no separate address to block.
  • Video ads stitched into the stream on the server, which arrive as the video itself.

It is also worth knowing that filter lists update when the app updates, not continuously. If a site has just changed how it serves ads, the next AdFence release is what closes the gap.

What the app should say

AdFence can ask Safari which of its extensions are actually running, and it is worth checking that the app and Safari agree. Open the app and tap Plugins. Each of the eight extensions gets a green Active when Safari reports it is enabled, and stays grey when it isn't. Refresh Status asks again, and Open Safari Extension Settings jumps straight to the screen shown above.

If Plugins shows a row as inactive while the switch in Safari looks on, the app is probably looking at a stale answer — tap Refresh Status before doing anything else.

Fix one site without loosening everything else

The usual reason someone turns a blocker off entirely is one site that breaks. There is a narrower option: open Site Settings for that address and use Pause Blocking. AdFence then stands down for that site alone, and every other site keeps the settings you chose.

Site Overrides goes one step further. Instead of pausing everything, it lets a single filter through for that one address — ad requests, tracking scripts, cookie notices — which is usually enough to make a stubborn site work without inviting the ads back everywhere.

If it still doesn't work

Two more things are worth checking before you write to us. First, make sure the app's own master Blocking switch on the General tab is on: while it is off, every extension loads an empty rule list and nothing is blocked anywhere, however Safari is set. Second, try the same page in a private window — if it behaves there and not in your normal window, the difference is a saved setting rather than the extension itself.

If none of that helps, email support@mxcode.com with the address of the page, what you expected to disappear, and your iOS or macOS version. A screenshot of the page is worth more than a description.

Frequently asked questions

Why did AdFence switch itself off after an update?

iOS and iPadOS updates occasionally reset Safari's extension settings, and Safari switches an extension off if the app was removed or reinstalled. Check Settings → Apps → Safari → Extensions on iPhone or iPad, or Safari → Settings → Extensions on the Mac, before assuming the app broke.

Do I need to enable all eight AdFence extensions?

No. Each extension covers a different category, and anything left off is simply not filtered — the others keep working.

Do I have to set it up again on each device?

Yes. Extensions are enabled per device, so an iPhone, an iPad and a Mac each need the extension switched on once.

Can I run AdFence alongside another ad blocker?

Safari can run more than one content blocker at once, and AdFence does not check for others. Two blockers aimed at the same requests mostly duplicate rules, so if a page misbehaves, switch one off to find out which.

Does it block YouTube or Instagram ads?

It blocks the ad slots served around a YouTube video, but not ads stitched into the video stream itself — those arrive as the video. Instagram and Facebook links open in their own in-app browsers, which no Safari extension can reach; open the page in Safari instead.
